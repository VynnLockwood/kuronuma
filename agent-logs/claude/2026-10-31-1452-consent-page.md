# A consent page people can decide on

- Agent: claude
- Date: 2026-10-31
- Branch / PR: feat/consent-page (PR link on GitHub)

## Goal
Owner, after connecting Gemini: the OAuth consent page "makes me feel stuck and guest-like, should I confirm it or not". The owner approved the design in chat (sketched inline), in English and Thai, including the read-only option.

## What was wrong
- No brand and no guidance; the only help was a warning that made a genuine request look like a phishing one.
- The signed-in email showed as `[email protected]`: Cloudflare's email obfuscation rewrites addresses in HTML into a link that needs a script, and the page's security policy forbids scripts.
- Approve always granted every scope the app asked for.

## What I did
- New `consent.go` holds the page, the English and Thai text, the grouping of scopes by area and the language choice; the old template and `renderConsent` moved out of `oauth.go`.
- The page: logo and wordmark, a question heading ("Connect Google to your Nemo?"), who is signed in, where the user lands afterwards, a "Check before you approve" box (approve if you just added Nemo in Gemini, Claude or ChatGPT; deny if you did not start this), the requested permissions grouped by area in everyday words (including that saving can create categories), an access level (Read and change, or Read only), and a note that the app name is not verified.
- Language: `?lang=th|en`, else the first of Thai or English in Accept-Language, else English; a visible switch keeps the rest of the request; a hidden field keeps the language through the form.
- Access level: `access=read` keeps only the `:read` scopes of the verified request; a missing value means full; an unknown value, or read-only for a request with no read scope, is refused. The read-only option is not offered when the request has no read scope. The granted scopes are logged.
- Email wrapped in `email_off` markers. CSP gains `img-src 'self'` (for the logo); still no scripts and no `form-action`, which would block the redirect back to the app.
- The error page shares the style and has the logo.
- `docs/design.md` consent paragraph.

## Verified
- Scratch harness (`gotest.sh`, Go 1.24 directive, `-race`): cerberus, hermes, themis, plutus, atlas all ok; vet clean; `gofmt -l apps/api` empty.
- Tests written first and seen failing: page content and grouping, only-what-was-requested, Thai and language choice and the switch link, the CSP, email markers, 7 access-level cases (full, none, read, read-all, unknown, write-only, scope list in the form ignored), a read-only code produces a read-only token, branded and escaped error page. Two existing assertions changed (the full-flow test now expects the new plain-language scope text).
- Rendered the real handler output to HTML and screenshot it in Chromium at desktop (1280px) and phone (390px) widths, English, Thai and the error page, with the real logo.

## Not verified / known gaps
- The logo in production is served by the web container at `/brand/nemo-logo.png`; in my test it was served from a local folder. nginx sends `/brand/` to the web container, so it should resolve, but I have not seen it on the live site.
- The Thai text is mine and needs a native speaker's read (listed in the PR). Error messages stay English.
- Cloudflare's setting itself was not changed: the markers stop the rewrite for this page; turning off "Email Address Obfuscation" in Cloudflare is optional.
- Go 1.26 and the `internal/store` and `cmd` compile are covered by CI only.

## Decisions and assumptions
- Changes the OAuth consent code, which needs the owner's approval under AGENTS.md; the owner approved the design in chat. The read-only option can only narrow access.
- No labels such as "verified Gemini" on the page: the page cannot prove who is asking, and a label would be copyable by a phishing client.
- The scope lines now say that saving can create categories (this covers the consent-text finding for this page; the plain `Description` in `main.go` is only a fallback for unknown scopes).
- Typographic apostrophes in the English text: a plain one in data would be HTML-escaped.
